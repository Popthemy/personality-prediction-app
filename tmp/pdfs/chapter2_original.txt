2.1 Conceptual Review 
2.1.1 The Big Five Personality Model (OCEAN) 
The Big Five Personality Model, commonly referred to as the OCEAN framework, is the most 
widely accepted and empirically validated model for describing human personality. The model 
categorizes personality into five broad dimensions: Openness to Experience, 
Conscientiousness, Extraversion, Agreeableness, and Neuroticism. These dimensions have 
been extensively validated across cultures and age groups, making them the standard 
framework for psychological assessment and computational personality prediction (Goldberg, 
1993; McCrae & Costa, 2008). 
Openness to Experience  describes an individual's curiosity, imagination, creativity, and 
willingness to explore new ideas. Individuals with high openness tend to enjoy abstract 
thinking, artistic expression, and intellectual discussions, whereas those with low openness 
generally prefer routine, familiarity, and conventional approaches. 
Conscientiousness reflects self -discipline, organization, reliability, and persistence in 
achieving goals. Highly conscientious individuals are dependable, structured, and 
achievement-oriented, while lower scores are associated with impulsiveness, poor planning, 
and disorganization. 
Extraversion represents sociability, assertiveness, enthusiasm, and positive emotionality. 
Individuals with high extraversion actively seek social interaction and external stimulation, 
whereas introverted individuals prefer solitude, reflection, and smaller social circles. 
Agreeableness measures compassion, cooperation, trust, empathy, and willingness to maintain 
harmonious relationships. Highly agreeable individuals are supportive and collaborative, while 
lower agreeableness is associated with competitiveness and skepticism.

Neuroticism, sometimes referred to as emotional instability, measures susceptibility to 
anxiety, stress, mood swings, and negative emotions. High neuroticism is associated with 
emotional instability, whereas low neuroticism reflects emotional resilience and psychological 
stability. 
The Big Five dimensions demonstrate strong predictive validity across numerous domains, 
including academic performance, job performance, leadership, interpersonal relationships, and 
mental wellbeing (Poropat, 2009; Barrick & Mount, 1991; Roberts et al., 20 07). Their 
consistency and interpretability make the model particularly suitable for automated personality 
prediction systems.

2.1.2 Digital Personality Footprints on Social Media 
Social media platforms have become valuable sources of behavioural data for computational 
personality analysis because they capture spontaneous and natural self -expression. Unlike 
traditional personality questionnaires, users continuously reveal aspects of  their behaviour, 
interests, communication style, and social interactions through their online activities. 
Among these platforms, X (formerly Twitter) provides rich multi-source information suitable 
for personality inference. Posts reflect linguistic style, emotional expression, topical interests,

and communication patterns. Profile information, including biographies, headlines, pinned 
posts, and self-descriptions, provides relatively stable indicators of personal identity and self -
presentation. Platform metadata, such as follower counts, following relationships, posting 
frequency, account age, and activity patterns, offers additional behavioural evidence. Network 
interactions, including mentions, replies, reposts, and community participation, further reveal 
users' social preferences and communication styles. 
Research has shown that combining textual, profile, behavioural, and network information 
significantly improves personality prediction compared with relying solely on textual content 
(Azucar et al., 2018; Farnadi et al., 2016; Tadesse et al., 2018). 
2.1.3 Social Media Signals for Big Five Personality Prediction 
Personality traits are reflected through different categories of digital signals on social media. 
These signals include textual content, profile information, metadata, and interaction patterns, 
each contributing complementary evidence for personality inference. 
Individuals with high openness frequently discuss abstract ideas, creative topics, innovation, 
literature, science, or philosophy. Their profiles often contain artistic or intellectual keywords, 
while their networks usually span diverse interests. 
Conscientiousness is commonly associated with structured posts, project updates, organized 
profile descriptions, professional photographs, regular posting behaviour, and stable long-term 
activity patterns. 
Extraversion is reflected through frequent conversations, event participation, mentions of 
other users, networking-oriented biographies, high posting activity, and extensive interaction 
with followers. 
Agreeableness is often indicated by supportive language, expressions of gratitude, 
collaborative profile descriptions, mentoring activities, and frequent replies to other users. 
Neuroticism may be observed through emotionally charged language, expressions of worry or 
uncertainty, inconsistent posting schedules, and unstable interaction patterns.

2.1.4 Applications of Personality Prediction 
Automated personality prediction has practical applications across multiple domains. 
1. Education

Educational institutions can use personality prediction to support adaptive learning, student

Conscientiousness has consistently been identified as one of the strongest predi ctors of

2009; Komarraju & Karau, 2008). 
2. Health and Wellbeing 
Personality prediction supports digital health monitoring by identifying behavioural tendencies

Organizations increasingly utilize personality assessment for recruitment, employee

Personality prediction also contributes to responsible AI by promoting transparency, fairness, 
and explainability in automated decision -making systems. Interpretable personality models 
assist in bias detection, fairness auditing, and ethical deployment of AI systems involving 
human behavioural analysis (Singh & Ramasubramanian, 2023; Sorokovikova et al., 2024).

2.2 Theoretical Framework 
2.2.1 Q-Learning for Active Text and Signal Selection 
Q-Learning is a model-free, off-policy reinforcement learning algorithm that learns an optimal 
action-value function without requiring a complete model of the environment. The core update 
rule is derived from the Bellman optimality equation and is expressed as: 
Q(s, a) ← Q(s, a) + α [r + γ max_{a′} Q(s′, a′) − Q(s, a)] 
Where:  
• (s) represents the current state (e.g., current prediction uncertainty or model 
confidence),  
• (a) denotes the action (e.g., selecting a specific post, bio segment, or metadata 
feature),  
• (r) is the immediate reward (e.g., improvement in prediction accuracy or 
reduction in uncertainty),  


=== PDF PAGE 31 ===
19 
 
• (α) is the learning rate controlling the update magnitude 
• (γ) is the discount factor balancing immediate versus future rewards (Sutton & 
Barto, 2018). 
In natural language processing and text classification tasks, Q-Learning has been successfully 
applied within active learning frameworks to intelligently select the most informative instances 
for labeling or processing. This selection reduces annotation cost and computational overhead 
while preserving or even improving model performance compared to random or passive 
sampling. The algorithm prioritizes samples that are expected to yield the greatest expected 
reward, typically measured through uncertainty reduction, expected model change, or expected 
error reduction (Thompson et al., 1999; Settles, 2012). 
In the present pipeline, Q -Learning forms the initial stage. It operates on multi -source social 
media signals from X posts, profile text (bio, headline, about section), metadata 
(followers/following counts, posting frequency, join date), and network featur es (interacted 
accounts, group memberships). The agent learns to select the subset of signals that maximizes 
downstream prediction accuracy while minimizing BERT processing cost. This capability is 
particularly valuable in employment-related applications where human resources professionals 
require rapid, resource -efficient screening of candidate profiles (McCallum & Nigam, 1998; 
Alhathli et al., 2020).

2.2.2 BERT for Contextual Semantic Embedding Extraction 
Bidirectional Encoder Representations from Transformers (BERT) is a transformer -based 
language model that pre -trains deep bidirectional representations by jointly conditioning on 
both left and right context in every layer. Pre -training objectives include m asked language 
modeling (predicting randomly masked tokens) and next sentence prediction, enabling the 
model to learn rich semantic and syntactic relationships (Devlin et al., 2019). 
The architecture consists of multiple transformer encoder layers with self -attention 
mechanisms that allow each token to attend to all other tokens in the input sequence. The output 
embeddings are highly contextualized: the representation of any given toke n depends on the 
entire surrounding context. For sentence -level tasks, the special [CLS] token embedding is 
commonly extracted as a fixed -length (typically 768 -dimensional in BERT -base) aggregate 
representation (Devlin et al., 2019). 
In personality prediction, BERT embeddings capture subtle linguistic markers —word choice, 
sentiment valence, syntactic complexity, topic coherence, and pragmatic intent—that correlate

21 
 
strongly with Big Five traits. The bidirectional context modeling is particularly advantageous 
for processing professional self-presentation in expressive content on X (Christian et al., 2021; 
Tsani & Suhartono, 2023; Majima & Markov, 2022). 
Within the pipeline, BERT constitutes the second stage. It generates high -quality contextual 
embeddings from the text segments and profile signals previously selected by the Q -Learning 
agent. These embeddings serve as the primary input for augmentation and final prediction.

2.2.3 Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) for Embedding Augmentation 
Generative Adversarial Networks (GANs) are deep learning models designed to generate 
synthetic data that closely resembles real data. A GAN consists of two neural networks trained 
simultaneously in a competitive process: a generator (G), which creates synthetic samples, 
and a discriminator (D), which attempts to distinguish between real and generated samples. 
During training, the generator continuously improves its  ability to produce realistic samples, 
while the discriminator becomes better at identifying synthetic ones. This adversarial learning 
process enables the generator to learn the underlying distribution of the training data 
(Goodfellow et al., 2014).

synthetic embeddings are combined with the original embeddings to increase the amount of 
training data while preserving the semantic characteristics learned by BERT. 
Embedding augmentation is especially beneficial when working with relatively small 
personality datasets, typically containing 80–150 participants  in controlled studies. By

2.3 Empirical Review 
The development of the proposed pipeline is informed by a growing body of empirical research 
that validates the effectiveness of BERT, GANs, Q-Learning, and Lasso regression in various 
natural language processing and personality inference tasks. This secti on provides a detailed 
explanation of works that have significantly shaped the methodology and theoretical focus of 
this study. 
Christian et al. (2021) investigated the use of large language model embeddings for personality 
trait prediction, utilizing Reddit comments from the PANDORA dataset. Their methodology 
involved evaluating LLM embeddings through a performance comparison with  zero-shot 
methods and conducting a psychometric validation by correlating embeddings with LIWC and 
emotion features. The results demonstrated that custom -trained deep learning models using 
LLM embeddings significantly outperformed zero -shot approaches by an average of 45% 
across all Big Five traits. They reported moderate reliability for the embeddings, with an 
average Cronbach’s alpha of 0.63, and found strong associations between specific traits and 
linguistic markers, such as the correlation between ope nness and social markers. This work 
establishes BERT and RoBERTa as robust semantic backbones for personality analysis. 
Tsani and Suhartono (2023) developed an ensemble model combining BERT and RoBERTa  
for personality prediction from Twitter and YouTube datasets. To address the challenge of class 
imbalance, they introduced a data augmentation method based on the T5 model, which used 
back-translation to generate paraphrased sentences. Their voting ensemble achieved an average 
F1 score of 0.730 on the Twitter dataset and 0.741 on the YouTube dataset. A key conclusion 
of their research was that the inclusion of data augmentation led to a measurable increase in 
average performance compared to models without such processing. Their study highlights the 
importance of using multiple transformer models and synthetic data to enhance the robustness 
of personality classifiers in noisy social media environments. 
Majima and Markov (2022) proposed a personality prediction system that combined BERT 
embeddings with statistical features extracted from text, such as uni -gram and bi -gram 

=== PDF PAGE 38 ===
26 
 
histograms, topic distributions, and emoticon usage. Their methodology employed a self -
attention mechanism to merge these heterogeneous information sources, allowing the model to 
dynamically prioritize the most relevant signals for each MBTI axis. Using 10 -fold cross -
validation on the Kaggle MBTI dataset, they demonstrated that the addition of statistical 
features slightly improved the performance of the base BERT system, increasing accuracy from 
83.4% to 84.2%. Their work demonstrates that while BERT is po werful for capturing 
contextual dependencies, task -specific statistical information provides critical user -level 
context that improves overall system performance. 
Hortal (2021) explored the use of GANs for data augmentation in the classification of 
imbalanced and scarce datasets, specifically focusing on EEG bio-signals. Their methodology 
utilized a Wasserstein GAN with a gradient penalty (WGAN-GP) to generate synthetic features 
that mirrored the distribution of real training data. They investigated different augmentation 
factors, combining the original dataset with synthetic sets of equal size to increase the total 
training volume. The results showed that the addition of GAN-generated samples provided a 
considerable enhancement in classification accuracy and helped mitigate overfitting. This 
study provides empirical justification for incorporating GAN -mediated augmentation in the 
current personality prediction pipeline to address the limitations of small datasets. 
Frid-Adar et al. (2018) focused on GAN-based synthetic data augmentation for medical image 
classification, using a limited dataset of CT images of liver lesions. 16 Their methodology 
involved synthesized high-quality lesion regions of interest using GAN architectures and then 
training a CNN with both classic and synthetic augmentation. The findings indicated that 
adding synthetic data increased sensitivity from 78.6% to 85.7% and specificity from 88.4% to 
92.4%. Their research demonstrates that GANs can effectively learn and replicate the essential 
features of a complex, high-dimensional data distribution, supporting the generalization of this 
technique to the augmentation of semantic embeddings in personality research. 
Antoniou et al. (2017) conducted a comprehensive survey and evaluation of various data 
augmentation techniques, including generative adversarial networks, for deep learning tasks.28

Their findings highlighted that GAN-based augmentation is an effective tool for dealing with 
small datasets, introducing greater diversity and complexity into the training samples than 
traditional geometric transformations. They concluded that GANs can capture subtle variations 
within the data, leading to more realistic and varied training samples that mimic real -world 
examples. This work establishes the foundational value of GANs as a regularizing mechanism 
that reduces generalization error in scenarios wh ere obtaining high -quality labeled data is 
costly or time-consuming. 
Thompson et al. (1999) investigated active learning for natural language parsing and 
information extraction, presenting a framework where the learner sequentially chooses the 
most informative unlabeled instances to be labeled. Their methodology focused on minimizing 
human annotation effort while producing as high -quality a classifier as possible. They 
demonstrated that active learning can significantly reduce the number of examples required for 
complex linguistic tasks. This research validates the inclusion of a selection mechanism in the 
proposed pipeline, specifically Q-Learning, to intelligently prioritize high-value text segments 
and metadata for processing, thereby achieving gains in computational efficiency. 
McCallum and Nigam (1998) introduced an algorithm for text classification from labeled and 
unlabeled documents using a combination of Expectation -Maximization (EM) and active 
learning. Their methodology showed that unlabeled data can significantly increase  
classification accuracy, particularly when labeled data is scarce. They proposed extensions to 
the basic EM scheme, such as dynamically weighting  the contribution of unlabeled data, to 
reduce bias and improve convergence. Their findings indicate that the combination of EM and 
active learning requires only slightly more than half as many labeled training examples to 
achieve the same accuracy as passive learning, providing strong support for the reinforcement-
based selection strategy utilized in this research. 
Settles (2012) provided a comprehensive review of active learning techniques, including 
uncertainty sampling and query-by-committee methods. The survey explained various practical 
challenges with active learning in real-world settings and highlighted how it can be combined

with diverse machine learning areas. The findings showed that uncertainty -based strategies 
consistently outperform random sampling by focusing on instances where the model makes 
unreliable predictions. This theoretical and empirical evidence supports the use of Q-Learning 
as the initial stage of the personality prediction pipeline, enabling the system to learn an optimal 
policy for signal prioritization based on prediction uncertainty. 
Tibshirani (1996) introduced the Lasso regression method, which combines linear regression 
with L1 -regularization to perform both variable selection and regularization. 18 The 
methodology assumes that the coefficients of the linear model are sparse, meaning that many 
of them are pushed exactly to zero. Tibshirani demonstrated that Lasso achieves both improved 
prediction accuracy and model interpretability by excluding irrelevant covariates from the 
model. This pioneering work serves as the basis for the final prediction layer of the proposed 
system, where Lasso id entifies the most influential 768 -dimensional BERT features for each 
personality trait, ensuring that the model outputs are explainable and auditable. 
Hastie et al. (2015) expanded upon the principles of statistical learning with sparsity, 
specifically evaluating Lasso and its generalizations in high -dimensional settings. Their 
research compared Lasso with other regularization techniques and confirmed it s superior 
performance in producing sparse, stable models when the number of features significantly 
exceeds the number of observations. They highlighted that Lasso’s ability to perform subset 
selection is fundamental for identifying informative features and simplifying complex models. 
This work validates the choice of Lasso for the final prediction stage, where interpretability is 
essential for the responsible application of personality prediction in sectors such as healthcare 
and employment. 
Poropat (2009) conducted a landmark meta -analysis of the relationship between the Big Five 
personality model and academic performance. The methodology involved synthesized results 
from numerous studies to establish the predictive validity of each personali ty dimension. The 
findings revealed that conscientiousness is the strongest and most consistent predictor of 
academic achievement, with a correlation of approximately 0.41. Openness was also found to

Rostami et al. (2022) investigated the association between Big Five personality traits and 
mental health in older adults, using observational data and psychometric scales.1 Their findings 
indicated that all five personality dimensions significantly predict mental health outcomes, with 
neuroticism exhibiting the strongest negative association. They reported that personality traits 
explain a substantial proportion of the variance in psychological wellbeing and resilience. This 
research underscores the clinical relevance of personality inference and demonstrates the 
potential of digital personality monitoring for supporting mental health interventions and 
wellbeing monitoring in the healthcare domain. 
Barrick and Mount (1991) performed a seminal meta-analysis on the relationship between the 
Big Five personality dimensions and job performance across various occupational categories. 
Their methodology involved categorizing job roles and performance criteri a to evaluate trait-
specific validity. The results established that conscientiousness is a valid predictor for all jobs, 
while extraversion and emotional stability (low neuroticism) are significant predictors for 
specific roles such as sales and management. Their findings have guided decades of personnel 
selection research and provide the empirical foundation for the employment -focused 
application of the integrated personality prediction pipeline.

involved assessing existing text-based systems against the principles of responsible AI. They 
highlighted that while deep learning models offer high accuracy, they often lack the 
transparency required for ethical deployment. The findings emphasized the need for 
interpretable models and bias -aware evaluation to ensure that personality profiling does not

2.4 Research Gaps 
The existing body of empirical and theoretical work on computational personality prediction 
has made considerable progress, yet several persistent and critical gaps remain that limit the 
development of accurate, efficient, interpretable, and practically de ployable systems for Big 
Five trait inference from social media data. These gaps are evident across methodological 
integration, data handling, interpretability, selection efficiency, and cross-domain applicability. 
Most published studies adopt a single -model or single -paradigm approach. Researchers 
typically fine-tune BERT or similar transformers on post text alone, or they apply classical 
feature engineering techniques such as LIWC and n-grams. Very few investigations attempt to 
combine reinforcement learning for active selection, deep contextual embeddings, generative 
augmentation, and sparse interpretable regression into a unified, end-to-end pipeline. No prior 
work has systematically integrated Q -Learning-driven s ignal selection, BERT -based 
representation, GAN-mediated augmentation, and Lasso regression specifically for personality 
prediction from heterogeneous social media sources that include posts, profile text, metadata, 
and network signals (Christian et al., 2 021; Tsani & Suhartono, 2023; Majima & Markov,

Data scarcity continues to represent a major constraint. Controlled academic datasets for

Interpretability remains severely underdeveloped in deep learning approaches. Transformer -
based models such as BERT deliver strong predictive performance but function as opaque 
black boxes. This lack of transparency creates significant barriers in high -stakes domains 
employment screening, mental health monitoring, educational advising, and algorithmic 
governance where stakeholders require clear, auditable explanations of which linguistic, 
profile, behavioral, or network signals drive each trait prediction. Sparse regression methods 
such as Lasso offer a proven path to interpretability through automatic feature selection and 
coefficient shrinkage, yet they are rarely combined with modern contextual embeddings in the 
personality prediction literature (Pletzer et al., 2025; Singh & Ramasubramanian, 2023; 
Tibshirani, 1996). 
Active selection mechanisms are underutilized in this domain. Reinforcement learning 
strategies, including Q-Learning, have demonstrated substantial reductions in annotation cost 
and computational overhead in general NLP tasks such as parsing, classificati on, and 
information extraction. However, their application to intelligently prioritize high-value signals 
from heterogeneous social media sources particularly when posts, bios, headlines, metadata, 
and network features are jointly available has received mi nimal attention. This represents a 
missed opportunity to achieve significant efficiency gains, especially in employment and 
education contexts where rapid, low-cost inference is essential (Thompson et al., 1999; Settles, 
2012; McCallum & Nigam, 1998).

2.5 Conceptual Framework 
The conceptual framework presents the overall architecture of the proposed personality 
prediction system. It integrates reinforcement learning, transformer -based natural language 
processing, generative data augmentation, and sparse regression into a unifie d pipeline for 
predicting the Big Five (OCEAN) personality traits from multi -source data collected from X 
(formerly Twitter). The framework demonstrates how information flows through successive 
processing stages, with each stage contributing a distinct fun ction toward producing accurate, 
robust, and interpretable personality predictions. 
2.5.1 Stage 1: Active Signal Selection 
The first stage collects multiple forms of user information from X, including posts, profile 
biography, profile metadata (such as follower count, following count, posting frequency, and 
account age), and network interactions. Since not all available information contributes equally 
to personality prediction, a Q -Learning agent is employed to identify and prioritize the most 
informative signals. This intelligent selection process reduces unnecessary processing while 
preserving relevant behavioural information required for subsequent analysis. 
2.5.2 Stage 2: Contextual Feature Extraction 
The selected textual information is passed to Bidirectional Encoder Representations from 
Transformers (BERT) for contextual feature extraction. Unlike traditional text representation 
methods, BERT captures the contextual meaning of words by considering bot h preceding and 
succeeding words within a sentence. This enables the model to generate rich semantic 

=== PDF PAGE 47 ===
35 
 
representations that effectively capture linguistic patterns associated with the Big Five 
personality traits. 
2.5.3 Stage 3: Embedding Augmentation 
The contextual embeddings generated by BERT are then supplied to a Conditional Generative 
Adversarial Network (cGAN) during the model training phase. Rather than generating 
synthetic social media posts, the cGAN  produces realistic embedding representations 
conditioned on the corresponding personality traits. This augmentation process increases the 
diversity of the training data, reduces overfitting, and improves the model's ability to generalize 
when only a limited number of labelled samples are available. 
2.5.4 Stage 4: Personality Prediction 
The final stage applies Lasso Regression to the original and augmented embedding 
representations. Lasso performs personality prediction while simultaneously selecting the most 
relevant features through L1 regularization. This produces interpretable predictions for the five 
personality dimensions Openness, Conscientiousness, Extraversion, Agreeableness, and 
Neuroticism while reducing the influence of redundant or less informative features. 
The integration of Q -Learning, BERT, Conditional GAN, and Lasso Regression forms a 
complete personality prediction framework that combines efficient feature selection, 
contextual language understanding, data augmentation, and interpretable prediction. The 
resulting personality profiles can support decision-making in education, health and wellbeing, 
employment, and responsible artificial intelligence applications.
